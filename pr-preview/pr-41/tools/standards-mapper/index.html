What it does

Aligning a state framework to a course is slow, and it is done by hand. This tool proposes the matches and a person confirms them. Every proposed match goes through review before it becomes a published claim. The reviewed output is the data layer that Standards Lookup reads from.

Results reach a district when somebody approves the run. That is the whole condition. Boundary notes arriving as drafts holds nothing back — REVIEW-DESIGN.md says why the old set-wide gate went.

Open questions

Settled, in the data contract

  • What happens to confirmed matches when a lesson changes underneath them? Every match stores the lesson's content fingerprint from the moment it was made. A scheduled job re-reads the curriculum and compares. Any match whose lesson changed goes back in the queue.
  • Should reviewers see the tool's confidence? No. A score invites a reviewer to trust it. Two named warnings replace it: Lesson may not go deep enough and Close match, likely not a real one. Each one says what to go and look at.
  • What holds results back from a district? An unapproved run, and nothing else. The set-wide boundary gate is gone.
  • One card at a time, or a list? A list. Working through forty standards one card at a time hid how much was left, and a reviewer could not skim for the flagged ones. The filter row above the queue replaces the paging.

Still open

  • Who can confirm a match, and does confirmation need a second reader?
  • Do we publish gaps, or only coverage? The public view returns the gaps either way. Somebody has to choose, in one place, whether to show them.
  • Which coverage percentage do we quote to a state — in scope, or whole framework? Both are on screen and both are true. The answer should be decided once, not per conversation.

The reasoning is in the briefing. The agreed shape of the data is in the contract.
